### Blocker Findings

*   **The Prisma + RLS Technical Credibility Gap (Voice & Authority):** Alex's core brand is "Technical Precision." Both Email 3 and Email 5 link to his guide on Prisma + PostgreSQL Row-Level Security as a friction-free silver bullet. However, any seasoned CTO or Systems Architect knows that Prisma has notorious, fundamental architectural friction with native PostgreSQL RLS (due to connection pooling, lack of native session-context support, and query engine limitations). Hand-waving this integration as a clean, 6-week drop-in solution without addressing the pooling/session state caveats will make a highly technical reader skeptical, instantly undermining Alex's positioning as a top-tier Systems Architect.
*   **The Welcome Drip vs. Tuesday Broadcast Collision (Operational Delivery):** There is a structural conflict in how the emails are delivered. If a subscriber is placed in this 21-day welcome sequence, do they *also* receive the live Tuesday broadcasts? 
    *   If **yes**, they will be double-emailed (e.g., receiving Email 2 on Monday and the live Tuesday Brief the next day), resulting in inbox congestion and unsubscribe risks. 
    *   If **no**, they miss the active weekly value for 3 weeks. 
    *   The copy in Email 4 ("Before I keep sending you architecture decisions every Tuesday...") assumes they have already been receiving the Tuesday Briefs, which is logically false if they are in a drip-onboarding quarantine.

### Major Findings

*   **Stage-Fit Quiz Context Blindness:** The sequence is triggered for Stage-Fit quiz takers but completely ignores their inputs. If a CTO just spent 3-5 minutes answering highly specific diagnostic questions about their system's architecture, zone, and pain points, receiving a completely generic "What's the biggest technical decision on your plate right now?" in Email 1 is jarring and lazy. It wastes the high-intent diagnostic data they just handed over.
*   **The Drip-Sequence Timeline Illusion:** Email 4 says: "Before I keep sending you architecture decisions every Tuesday..." and "Short email this week." Because this onboarding sequence is on a relative delay (Days 0, 3, 7, 14, 21), these emails will land on arbitrary days of the week based on sign-up date (e.g., a subscriber signing up on Thursday gets them on Thursday, Sunday, Thursday, Thursday). Referring to "every Tuesday" and "this week" breaks the "fourth wall" of automation for highly analytical readers who immediately recognize the drip mechanics.
*   **Redundant Asset Linking:** Email 3 and Email 5 both feature the exact same P.S. link: *Multi-Tenancy with Prisma and Row-Level Security*. Reusing the same deep-dive link twice in a 5-email sequence makes the content library feel small and overly obsessed with a single technical pattern (RLS).

## Executive Summary

1. **llms.txt is cosmetic, not functional (2026).** Major AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Meta-ExternalAgent) do not request it in production logs. One audit: 0.1% of AI bot hits across 3 months. Zero correlation between llms.txt presence and citation frequency. Keep the file for completeness, but do not rely on it.[1][4]
2. **The real ranking surface is three indexes: Bing, Brave, Google.** ChatGPT Search is Bing-index-dependent (pages not in Bing are invisible; 87–92% overlap with Bing SERPs). Claude.ai web search uses Brave (86.7% citation overlap). Google AI Overviews use Google's index. Perplexity runs its own hybrid index, but heavy cross-reference with Bing/Google. Meta AI pulls from Bing + open web.[2][3][9][13]
3. **Content structure beats domain authority for citation.** 47–83% of AI-Overview citations come from pages ranking below position 5. Princeton/Georgia Tech GEO paper (KDD 2024, peer-reviewed) shows **adding statistics (+32–41%), citing authoritative sources (+30–40%), and quotations (+15–30%)** are the highest-leverage edits, measured by Position-Adjusted Word Count.[5][6]
4. **Extractability is the single biggest lever.** Answer capsules (130–160 words, self-contained, leading with direct answer) + inverted-pyramid subsections cite at ~65% higher rates than dense prose. AI Overviews extract 134–167-word passages preferentially. Content that reads cleanly as chunk-level vectors wins.[7][8]
5. **Measurement is partially solved.** Bing Webmaster Tools shipped AI Performance Report (Feb 2026) — shows Copilot citations + grounding queries. GA4 requires custom channel group (regex on source). ChatGPT applies `utm_source=chatgpt.com` automatically on desktop, but mobile-app referrers strip — 20–40% of AI traffic misattributes to direct. No GSC-equivalent for ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity yet.[10][11][12]

## Per-Platform Breakdown

### ChatGPT (browsing / SearchGPT)

- **Crawler:** OAI-SearchBot (indexing), ChatGPT-User (live fetch), GPTBot (training).
- **Index:** Own SearchGPT index, but **heavily bootstrapped from Bing's index**. 87–92% citation overlap with Bing top results. Google used as occasional fallback.[9][13]
- **Citation rate:** Only ~15% of retrieved pages appear in the final answer; 85% are retrieved and discarded.[2]
- **Key signal:** Bing rank. If your page is not in Bing's index, it cannot be cited.
- **Referrer:** `chatgpt.com` with `utm_source=chatgpt.com` on desktop; mobile-app strips.[11]

### Claude.ai (web_search tool)

- **Backend:** **Brave Search**, confirmed via Anthropic's subprocessor list and `BraveSearchParams` parameter. 86.7% overlap between Claude citations and Brave top results.[3]
- **Crawlers:** ClaudeBot (training), Claude-User (live), Claude-SearchBot (search quality). All three clarified in Feb 2026 docs.[3]
- **Mechanics (Feb 2026):** `web_search_20260209` tool added "dynamic filtering" — Claude runs Python code to pre-filter raw HTML before it enters context. Implication: pages with heavy chrome/JS will have content extracted less cleanly than pages with dense semantic HTML.[14]
- **Key signal:** Brave rank — which is closer to content relevance + freshness than backlinks. Unusual advantage: mid-tier domains can rank for technical long-tail.
- **Referrer:** `claude.ai` — no UTM.

### Perplexity

- **Index:** Own Sonar hybrid index ("hundreds of billions of webpages"). Combines semantic, LLM ranking, human feedback. Not Bing/Google dependent.[15]
- **Behavior:** Visits ~10 pages per query, cites 3–4. Extraction-first: picks the page it can most cleanly lift from.[8]
- **Top signals (independent audit):** citation frequency (35%), visual citation placement (20%), domain authority (15%), schema markup (10%), security (5%).[8]
- **Freshness is aggressive:** content update → +37% citation in 48 hours (controlled test). Date-stamp everything.[8]
- **Referrer:** `perplexity.ai`, UTMs frequently.

### Google AI Overviews / AI Mode

- **Index:** Google's own. Pulls from ranked organic but de-emphasizes top-10: **38% of citations from pages in top 10** (down from 76% in mid-2025); 47% come from positions 6+.[5]
- **Signals:** semantic completeness (score 8.5+/10 → 4.2× more likely cited), fact-verification (89% boost when stats cross-check with authoritative databases), entity density (15+ recognized entities → 4.8× citation).[5]
- **Extraction window:** 134–167-word passages preferred; 62% of cited chunks 100–300 words.[5]
- **Referrer:** `google.com` — AI-specific referrer not distinguishable from organic today.

### Bing Copilot

- **Index:** Bing's own, RAG pipeline.
- **Interesting quirk:** cites pages not indexed by Google Search — Bing maintains a separate AI index.[4]
- **Measurement (NEW):** Bing Webmaster Tools **AI Performance Report** (public preview, Feb 2026) — shows Copilot citations + grounding queries per URL. First vendor-native AI citation telemetry.[10]
- **Signals:** recency, structured headings, verified authorship, schema.

### Meta AI

- Thin public documentation. Crawler: Meta-ExternalAgent (already allowed in robots). Uses Bing API + open web. Citation behavior opaque; assume Bing-index dependent.

## Content Patterns — Pull-Through Rate

**High pull-through (cite these patterns):**

- **Statistical claims with dated, primary sources.** +32–41% citation boost. "As of Feb 2026, ChatGPT cites 15% of retrieved pages [OpenAI system card]."[6]
- **Definitional content** (coined terms, glossary entries) — if concise (25–75 words), standalone, and schema-marked with `DefinedTerm`. alexmayhew.dev's Cognitive Debt / Generative Debt glossary is on the right axis.
- **How-to with numbered steps** — LLMs extract enumerated lists cleanly for procedural queries.
- **Comparison tables** — 2-column dense facts extract well; the GEO paper specifically showed tables in citations.
- **Code snippets with language tags** — shown verbatim in technical answers; the surrounding prose gets cited back.
- **Expert quotations** (+28%) — even your own quotes, if attributed with name + title. Perplexity and Claude preferentially cite quoted human speakers.

**Low pull-through:**

- Pure opinion without supporting numbers.
- Rambling anecdotes (long narrative without extractable claim).
- Purely visual content (diagrams with image-only captions — LLMs can't read alt text for citations).
- SEO-keyword-stuffed headers ("Best SaaS Architecture Frameworks in 2026 | Top 10 Guide") — pattern-matched as low-trust.

## Attribution & Citation Mechanics

- **Canonical URL gets cited when:** Article schema has explicit `url`, `mainEntityOfPage.@id` matches canonical, `og:url` matches canonical, and the HTML `<link rel="canonical">` matches. One mismatch = LLM may pick OG title or any URL it retrieved. alexmayhew.dev has canonical discipline per `nextjs-canonical-url-best-practices-2026.md` — verify it's consistently applied to all MDX articles.
- **`sameAs` is meaningful** for entity disambiguation, _especially_ when your entity could be confused with others. Cite 3.2× attribution improvement claim is vendor-sourced (unverified in peer review) but mechanistically aligned with how Google Knowledge Graph works — LLMs use KG-derived entity signals via pre-training and RAG-time retrieval.[16]
- **DOI-style permanent IDs are NOT worth it for blog posts.** DOIs matter in academia because citation managers use them. LLMs don't parse DOI tags; they use URLs. Stable slug + redirect discipline (301 on rename) achieves the same.
- **Author authority signals:** Article schema `author.@id` pointing to a Person with dense `sameAs` + `hasCredential` + clear bio. alexmayhew.dev does this — it's the right model.

## Ranking Traps to Avoid

1. **FAQ spam.** SE Ranking audit: pages with FAQ schema cited at 3.6 avg vs 4.2 without. Only use FAQ on genuinely Q&A content. Do not retrofit every page.[18]
2. **Thin glossary entries** — a 10-word definition schema-marked as DefinedTerm reads as grift. Each glossary entry should be 50–150 words with an example.
3. **Over-stuffing JSON-LD** — pages with 8+ schema types on a single URL trigger ambiguity; LLMs tokenize JSON-LD as raw text and dilute signal. Pick the 2–3 schemas that match the page's primary entity.
4. **llms.txt bloat** — keep llms.txt compact. llms-full.txt can be long, but if it's truly huge (>100KB), no crawler will fetch it anyway.
5. **Over-optimization** — stuffing keywords, inflating word counts past ~2000 for topics that don't warrant it. 2026 Google core update (March) was volatile specifically on over-optimized content.[20]
6. **Blocking AI crawlers "to protect training"** — this also blocks citation. If you want to block training but allow search: robots.txt allow OAI-SearchBot + block GPTBot (OpenAI honors this split). Same pattern for Anthropic: allow Claude-SearchBot/Claude-User, block ClaudeBot. alexmayhew.dev allows all — this is the right choice for maximum visibility, since the goal is citation, not training-data exclusion.
7. **Mobile-unfriendly or JS-heavy renders** — Claude's Feb 2026 dynamic filtering downgrades pages that need JS to show content. Server Components (Next.js 15 default for this project) are the correct choice. Verify no critical content requires client-side hydration.

## Conflicting Information

- **Schema markup impact:** BrightEdge claims +44%; SE Ranking audit finds FAQ schema slightly _negative_; GEO paper (peer-reviewed) doesn't isolate schema as a variable. Verdict: **schema correctly applied is modestly positive or neutral; schema misapplied (FAQ spam) is negative.** Go narrow and accurate, not broad.
- **Domain authority correlation:** Some sources cite 0.18 correlation for AIO; others call it foundational for Perplexity (15% weight). Likely both true — Perplexity values domain authority more than Google AIO does.
- **llms.txt:** Vendor-sponsored posts (ALLMO, Bluehost, etc.) promote it. Independent audits (Longato 2025, Otterly 2026) show ~0.1% fetch rate. **Trust the audits.**
